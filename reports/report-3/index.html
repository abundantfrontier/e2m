Preference optimization, a second 7B, and the validation gap

Technical report 3 of the E2M program: a DPO run from lora2 whose generation collapsed, the repair loop on the validation set, and a second 7B QLoRA trained on the lora2 mix, compared across all models so far and against the NL-to-C-to-gcc control.

Boyd Wilson, Abundant Frontier Institute and Omnibond Systems

Living paper · Revision 1 · Last updated 16 November 2026 · Companion to the E2M concept paper, technical report 1 and technical report 2 · All results are from one 24 GB RTX 3090 workstation · Code and data: github.com/abundantfrontier/e2m

Working paper. You may cite it; see How to cite. Numbers are copied from runs/round2b/SUMMARY.md, runs/round2b/RESULTS.json, runs/round2/SUMMARY.md, runs/round2/RESULTS.json, STATUS.md and the training files under runs/dpo and runs/qlora7b-v2 on the 3090; nothing here is re-scored. Abstract

Report 2 left three things undone: the repair loop on the validation set, preference optimization (DPO) on the 46,472 compiler preference pairs, and a 7B baseline. This report covers them. The lora2 repair loop on the 90 validation tasks passes 60/90 (k = 8, R = 3), up from 49/90 for lora1. DPO started from lora2 (beta 0.1, 5,574 steps) reached a pair accuracy of 0.902 on 512 held-out pairs, but its generation collapsed: greedy pass@1 is 3/90 on validation and 0/78 on held-out, and 83 of 90 and 76 of 78 greedy outputs do not assemble. We offer hypotheses for the collapse; we did not test any of them. A second 7B QLoRA (qlora7b-v2), trained on the same 6,781-row mix as lora2, is the strongest single-try model on validation (41/90 greedy, 52/90 pass@5) and on held-out pass@5 (12/78), ties qlora7b at 5/78 greedy on held-out, and passes 12/78 on held-out with the repair loop (17/78 on visible tests only). The 7B base model without an adapter passes 0/78 greedy and 3/78 at pass@5. Validation and held-out remain far apart: greedy pass@1 is 0.456 on validation and 0.064 on held-out for qlora7b-v2, and the NL-to-C-to-gcc control still reaches 31/78 greedy and 44/78 with its repair loop. Round 2 and round 2b used 40.68 of 60 additional GPU hours. All numbers are copied from files on the 3090.

1Goals recap updated 16 Nov 2026

Report 2 added a visible-test repair loop and one round of rejection sampling, which produced lora2. It ended with a list of unfinished work: the validation loop (loop-lora2-val), DPO on data/scaled/pairs.jsonl, and a 7B baseline. Its next-step list proposed a second rejection round or DPO gated on held-out correctness. This report reports what happened when the pipeline was resumed under a new budget: 60 additional GPU hours and a deadline of 18 November 2026, 18:00 Pacific time. The pipeline ran in two parts. Round 2 ran the validation loop, DPO training, DPO evaluation and the DPO held-out loop, and halted at a stage cap. Round 2b resumed it and ran the 7B stages.

Terms follow Reports 1 and 2. pass@1 is greedy. pass@5 is the fraction of tasks with at least one passing sample out of five (T = 0.8). A repair loop samples k candidates and repairs for up to R rounds using the visible tests only; scoring uses the full tests. The validation set is 90 scaled tasks from the training distribution, and the held-out set is 78 HumanEval and MBPP tasks.

3.3Generation collapsed updated 16 Nov 2026

Generation did not follow the training signal. The DPO model passes 3 of 90 validation tasks greedily (0.033; pass@5 4/90) and 0 of 78 held-out tasks (0.000; pass@5 0/78). That is below every model in Reports 1 and 2, including lora2, the model it started from (38/90 and 4/78). Of the 90 validation greedy outputs, 83 do not assemble, 3 are wrong answers, 3 pass and 1 crashes. Of the 78 held-out greedy outputs, 76 do not assemble and 2 are wrong answers. Among the sampled outputs (five per task), 419 of 450 validation samples and 381 of 390 held-out samples do not assemble.

The failures are visible in the text. In a tally of runs/dpo/eval.jsonl, 70 of the 90 validation greedy outputs and 73 of the 78 held-out greedy outputs contain a run of stray % tokens (the substring % % %); the same tally gives 0 of 90 and 0 of 78 for lora2 and for qlora7b-v2. The first assembler error is most often bad register name (45 of the 83 non-assembling validation outputs and 43 of the 76 held-out ones; first error line of each output, a tally of the file), followed by junk after a register or expression and no such instruction. One validation output starts with a plausible .text, .globl and .type header, then degenerates into lines such as sall %cl, %eax, %const-15 and rows of %. The few outputs that assemble and pass are close to the gcc reference (size and instruction ratios of 1.000 over n = 3).

3.4Loop on held-out and the stage cap updated 16 Nov 2026

The DPO held-out repair loop (loop-dpo-heldout, k = 8, R = 3) ran under a 10-hour stage cap (--max-hours 10, the pipeline's stage allowance for this loop) and stopped at 10.03 GPU hours after 62 of the 78 tasks. It passes 0 of 62 on the full tests and on the visible tests, all 62 final attempts are doesnt_assemble, and the mean number of calls is 32.0, so every task used its whole budget. The stage ended with code 3 ("deadline or max-hours"), which halted round 2 before the 7B stages. Round 2b resumed the pipeline and skipped this loop rather than continue it (runs/round2b/SUMMARY.md: "resume does not rerun or continue loop-dpo-heldout"). The 62-task result is therefore incomplete, and it is not comparable to the 78-task rows; but with 62 of 62 final attempts failing to assemble, we do not expect the remaining 16 tasks to change the picture.

3.5Possible causes (hypotheses, not tested) updated 16 Nov 2026

We ran one DPO configuration, evaluated only its final adapter, and logged the pair margin but not the log-probability of the chosen and rejected responses separately. We cannot therefore say which of the following explains the collapse; they are candidates for the next round (§9).

  1. Likelihood displacement. The DPO loss depends on the difference between the chosen and rejected log-ratios. It can be driven down while the likelihood of both responses falls. The loss in dpo.py has no supervised term on the chosen response to anchor it. A pair accuracy of 0.902 says that chosen outranks rejected; it says nothing about the absolute probability of well-formed assembly.
  2. Near-identical pairs. Both responses in a pair are verified-correct programs for the same task, differing in size or instruction count. In a pair like the first row of pairs.jsonl (the same function compiled two ways), most tokens are shared. The gradient then acts on a few differing tokens and on tokens common to both, and the model may lower the probability of syntax that appears in both.
  3. Distribution shift from gcc-style output. The pairs draw on gcc, clang, rustc and llvm-opt variants, whereas lora2 was trained on gcc-default assembly. Clang-style and AVX-encoded text (for example vcvtsi2ss with an %xmm15 operand, in the first pair) is a different surface form from the SFT data.
  4. Goal mismatch. About two thirds of the pairs are labeled size or speed (30,169 of 46,472), but every training prompt says Goal: default. The model was asked to prefer size- or speed-optimized text under a prompt that does not mention either.
  5. Aggressive optimization settings. A learning rate of 5e-5 over 5,574 steps with a logged per-step reward margin of 7.19 at the last step (step 5,574; 4.44 on the held-out pairs) and no checkpoint evaluation along the way. We do not know when generation broke. A smaller learning rate, a larger beta or earlier stopping might avoid it.
  6. A mismatch between the pair objective and the task. The pairs come from the verified (passing) variants of the sweeps and rank two programs by size or instruction count; we did not check every pair, but we did not construct any with a non-assembling or wrong-answer program on the rejected side. If so, the objective never told the model to prefer assembling code over code that fails. Round 2's own next step proposed pairs "gated on held-out correctness", which this run did not do.

4.1The base 7B without an adapter updated 16 Nov 2026

Report 1 could not report a 7B base baseline because the run was cut off at 28 of 78 tasks, and the first round 2 summary again showed only 0/28 greedy and 2/28 at pass@5. Round 2b completed it (7b-base-asm, 0.31 GPU hours with the cached 28 tasks reused; Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct, 4-bit, no adapter). From lora1 to qlora7b-v2, validation greedy pass@1 more than doubles (0.211 to 0.456) while held-out moves from 0.026 to 0.064 (2/78 to 5/78). The loop shows the same shape: lora2 reaches 60/90 on validation and 10/78 on held-out. The held-out set is small, so single-task differences are noise (one task is 0.013), and the held-out improvements from lora1 to qlora7b-v2 are a few tasks.

What the gap implies, with the evidence available:

  1. The models fit the validation distribution. The training and validation tasks come from the same scaled corpus, and lora2 and qlora7b-v2 were additionally trained on rejection-sampled programs for training tasks from that corpus. Gains from more data of that kind (rewrites, rejection sampling, a larger model) show up on validation first. We read the validation numbers as in-distribution fit, not as evidence of transfer, as Reports 1 and 2 did. The gap itself does not tell us whether it comes from a distribution difference (library-style functions against HumanEval/MBPP puzzles), from memorization of near-duplicates, or from both.
  2. Held-out failures are different failures. On validation, qlora7b-v2 fails mostly by wrong answer (39 of 90) and rarely by not assembling (6); on held-out, 11 of 78 do not assemble and 14 time out, and the wrong answers are 40. We have not inspected the held-out wrong answers by cause.
  3. Do not select models on validation alone, or on pair accuracy. The best model on validation (qlora7b-v2) is also the best or tied on held-out, but the margins on held-out are a few tasks. DPO shows that a training metric can mislead: it looked good on pair accuracy and was the worst model on both sets. A held-out set of 78 tasks is a coarse selector. A larger or fresher held-out set, with confidence intervals, would be a better guide than validation.
  4. The C control is the reference. The same family of model, asked to write C and compiled by gcc, passes 31/78 greedy. The task set is solvable; the gap to the control is in producing correct assembly directly.

8Limitations and caveats updated 16 Nov 2026

  1. Small held-out set. At 78 tasks, 5/78 against 4/78 is not a difference. No confidence intervals are reported and no run was repeated with a different seed.
  2. One DPO configuration. We ran DPO once, with one beta, learning rate and data mix, and evaluated only the final adapter. The collapse says this configuration failed; it does not say DPO fails on this task.
  3. The DPO causes in §3.5 are untested. We did not log chosen and rejected log-probabilities separately, and did not run an ablation.
  4. The DPO held-out loop is incomplete. It covers 62 of 78 tasks and stopped at its 10-hour stage cap; it was not resumed.
  5. Loop budgets are not matched. The C loop uses k = 4, R = 2; the assembly loops use k = 8, R = 3. There is no loop result for qlora7b or base7b, and none for qlora7b-v2 on validation.
  6. Loop and single-try results answer different questions. A loop uses up to 32 model calls and test feedback (§5).
  7. Ratios over few passes. Size and instruction ratios are means over passing tasks only, with n between 0 and 60, and the subsets differ between rows.
  8. No in-training eval loss for lora2 or qlora7b-v2 (val_tasks=0), because the mix already excluded the validation and held-out ids.
  9. Tallies computed for this report. The DPO error and % % % counts in §3.3, the DPO log averages in Table 3 and the pair-goal counts in §3.1 and §3.5 are tallies of eval.jsonl, loss.jsonl and pairs.jsonl, not figures in the summaries. The % % % count is a substring heuristic.
  10. Rewritten specifications. The human check of a sample of rewritten specifications is still outstanding (Report 1, §9; Report 2, §8).
  11. Tests. The pytest suite was not re-run for this report.

9Proposed next steps updated 16 Nov 2026

proposed None of the following has been started. Each is a proposal for discussion, and none is a commitment.

  1. Diagnose the DPO collapse before dropping DPO. Log chosen and rejected log-probabilities separately, add a supervised term on the chosen response, lower the learning rate and raise beta, and evaluate intermediate checkpoints. This tests hypotheses 1, 5 and 6 of §3.5.
  2. Rebuild the preference pairs. Use pairs whose rejected side fails (does not assemble or gives a wrong answer) against a passing chosen side, restrict to gcc-style output, and match the prompt goal to the pair goal. This tests hypotheses 3, 4 and 6.
  3. A second rejection-sampling round from qlora7b-v2, which is the strongest model so far and passes slightly more held-out tasks than lora2 under the loop (12/78 against 10/78). Measure it on held-out first, not on validation.
  4. Run the validation loop for qlora7b-v2 so Table 8 has a matched validation row for the 7B.
  5. A larger or fresher held-out set with confidence intervals, and repeated seeds for the headline rows, so that differences of a few tasks can be judged.
  6. Inspect the held-out wrong answers and timeouts of qlora7b-v2 by cause (for example, wrong calling convention, off-by-one in a loop, missed edge case) to see whether there is a pattern a data fix could address.
  7. Close the gap to the control. Held-out is qlora7b-v2 loop at 12/78 against 44/78 for the C loop. One option is to use the C control's compiled output as a training source for the same specifications.
  8. Human check of a sample of rewritten specifications, as in Reports 1 and 2.
